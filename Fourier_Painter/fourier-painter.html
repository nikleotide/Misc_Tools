How it works. Your stroke is resampled into 512 points evenly spaced along its length, so travelling once around the shape takes t from 0 to 1. That gives one periodic signal per axis, and each is run through a discrete Fourier transform to get an amplitude and a phase for every harmonic k. Rebuilding a signal means adding those sines back up: each rotating vector is one sine term, and its shadow on the axis is that term's contribution.

Sound. A recording is a wave, and one slice of it is one period. The app finds the repeating cycle by autocorrelation, resamples it to 512 points, and decomposes it exactly as it does a drawing — except there is only one axis, so there is only one stack of vectors. Play the window against the rebuilt version to hear what a given number of harmonics keeps.

Image. The picture's background colour is estimated from its border, everything far enough from it becomes the subject, the largest connected region is kept, and its outer edge is walked pixel by pixel into a closed loop. That loop is a drawing like any other.

Scan mode. Trace draws in parameter order, the way the pen actually moves. Scan sweeps a line across the shape's own x axis instead and marks every y the curve passes through at that column — two of them wherever the shape doubles back, more where it folds again. It is the same series either way; only the order of arrival changes.

In space. The plan view sets x(t) and y(t); the height strip sets z(t) as the pen travels the loop. Three vector stacks, one per axis, meet at the pen.
